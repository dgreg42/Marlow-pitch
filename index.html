Somewhere you want to be at six in the morning.

An hour in your own house that belongs to nobody else. We design the room it happens in, and everything that goes in it.

Speak to someone What a room costs

The door is heavy. The room behind it is quieter than the rest of the house, which is most of the point.

Forty minutes with the bar. Then heat, then thirty seconds of cold water you will dread and do anyway. Then a chair, and nothing asked of you.

Then you go back up, and the rest of the house wakes up.

That is one morning, in one room, for one person. We start with yours.

Who it is for

Nobody uses the room the same way.

The first question is never what you want in the room. It is what you already do in a week.

You lift, four days a week

The platform, the bar and the plates are the room, and everything else gets out of the way. Whatever cardio you will actually use.

You run, and pay for it the next day

Heat and cold earn their place here ahead of another machine. The bench is mostly for sitting on.

You want twenty minutes nobody can reach you

Then the room is a chair, a window, a warm floor and a door that closes properly. The rest is optional.

Two of you, and one of you does not lift

It has to work for both without either one apologising for it. Usually that is two zones and one door rather than two rooms.

The room

One room, two rooms, or a floor.

What the house will carry decides more than taste does, and we tell you which it is before anything is drawn. On the wet side, water and power are the whole conversation.

Where the work happens

A platform that takes a dropped bar quietly. Oak and blackened steel in place of powder coat. Storage that does not look like storage.

Where the water is

Sauna, cold plunge and steam, in whatever combination the house will take. Honed limestone, linen, and somewhere to sit when you are done.

A chair, a window, and a towel that was warm when you got to it.

The pieces

Everything in here has a reason.

The rack, the bench and the wall the equipment hangs on are drawn for your house. Everything else we buy from people who already make it well.

On handover you get a page that says why each piece is the one we picked, and what we turned down.

Materials

Every material in here ages well.

Limewash plaster, rift sawn white oak, honed limestone, western red cedar, wool, linen, leather, waxed iron.

No powder coat, no vinyl, no moulded plastic. Filtered water in the room, and something green and alive in every one.

A room has a smell whether anybody chose it or not, so we choose it. Cedar and leather come from the room itself. Eucalyptus in the steam and hinoki on the sauna bench, if that is what you want in there.

How it works

Four steps, two of them yours.

  1. An hour with you

    Not what you want in the room. What you already do in a week, who else in the house will use it, and what you have given up on.

  2. A drawing and one price

    Plans, elevations, a render, and a written reason for every piece.

  3. The page your builder needs

    Circuits, amperage, blocking and floor boxes, in with the rough in.

  4. Our crew, then a morning

    Installed into a finished house, walked through with you, finished inside forty eight hours.

Care

Somebody has to look after the water.

Sauna heater and stones, steam seals, plunge chemistry and filters, the equipment, and the technology. On a schedule, for as long as you own the room.

Second homes are opened and closed for the season. No owner is named, and nothing is published without your permission in writing.

Questions

The ones we get asked.

I do not really know what I want.

That is the normal place to start. We work from the week you already have rather than from a brief you had to write.

Do you work with my builder and my designer?

Yes, and it is the easier way to do it. We work to the builder's site rules and give the designer documents she can put in her own presentation.

Can my house take a sauna and a plunge?

Sometimes, and it is the first thing we check. A steam generator can want 125 amps on its own, so this is settled before anything is drawn.

Do you make money on the equipment?

Yes. The rack can come from one company and the sauna from another, and we make a margin on what we supply. Every piece comes with the reason we chose it over the others.

Will anybody know it is my house?

No. No owner is named, and nothing is photographed or published without your permission in writing.

Tell us what you actually do in a week.

Not equipment, not square footage. We will tell you what the house can carry and roughly what it would cost, before anybody draws anything.

The gap

Nobody has put the pieces in one place.

The pieces all exist. Nobody has put them in one place: both rooms, one studio, our own crew, a rack from one company and a sauna from another, a written reason for every piece, and a care plan that covers the sauna and the plunge, not just the treadmill. Selling through the trade rather than a showroom is the sixth, and it is the one our own research supports least.

The two that matter most are the written reason and the care plan. One is a trust argument nobody else can make without changing their business. The other is the difference between what this company is worth at four times earnings and at seven.

  • Every firm in the category writes the same way. Sanctuary, bespoke, elevate, elegance, one roof, world class. We wrote our refusal list before this research and it turned out to be an accurate map of the category's vocabulary.
  • Nobody services the sauna or the plunge. Water chemistry, heaters, seals, drainage, cedar. Those systems degrade and the owner cannot fix them. That is exactly where the repeat revenue sits.
  • Nobody has said out loud that they earn on what they specify. Every buyer in this market is quietly wondering, and nobody has answered.

The systems that need looking after are the ones nobody else will service.

Electrical

A steam room requires 125 amps on its own.

That is the big generator. The small one still wants 38. A traditional sauna heater wants 30 to 50 on a circuit of its own. A commercial treadmill needs one it does not share with anything, including its neutral. The plunge needs a GFCI line and no extension cord, ever. Our read is that a full suite adds up to its own subpanel, though no code sets that threshold and we would have the electrician confirm it on the house.

Sauna, plunge and red light off one corridor. Each is its own line on the technology sheet.

So the electrical gets drawn before the framer arrives. Every room leaves the builder with a one page technology sheet: circuits and amperage, where the subpanel goes, floor boxes under the cardio, blocking in the wall for the display and the speakers, where the low voltage runs. Anything missed at that moment costs far more after the drywall. Dallas has been on the 2023 electrical code since May 2025, and the park cities are a separate check every time.

That timing is why the audio visual companies matter to us. We draw the technology and one of their certified installers puts it in, because programming a house's control system is their trade. They are in these houses long before we would be, and nobody in this category is courting them.

It gives the care plan a fifth thing to service. Firmware, network, speakers and scenes, on the same visit as the heater and the water chemistry.

Where we stand

Five things we decided before we started.

  • Several brands, not one. We hold dealer agreements with a number of manufacturers, so the rack can come from one and the sauna from another. Nobody has to take a package.
  • We make money on the equipment we sell. That goes in writing beside every piece, with the reason it is in the room, so an owner can tell whether it is there for them or for us.
  • Both rooms, one studio. The gym and the wellness room, one crew, and one care plan covering both.
  • We sell through designers and builders. They are drawing the house a year before anybody thinks about equipment, and that is when this gets decided.
  • Lighter and warmer than most gyms. The room has to sit beside the interior designer's work without fighting it.
Craft

Gym equipment is the only furniture in the house nobody expects to look at.

Everything else in the house got chosen. The chairs, the lighting, the door handles, the stone on the island. Then the gym turns up in black powder coat and goes in the basement behind a door.

Make it worth looking at and the room moves upstairs, beside the living space behind glass. Then it gets used, because you walk past it forty times a day instead of deciding to go down there.

So a rack gets drawn the way furniture gets drawn. Oak uprights sized to the wall rather than to a catalogue. Steel welded, ground and blackened by hand, because powder coat chips and blackened steel just wears. Hardware countersunk so nothing catches a knuckle.

Most of the drawing time goes on transitions. Every detail below is one, and you are not supposed to notice any of them.

Anything we can buy well, we buy. The pieces that decide how the room feels, the rack, the bench, the wall the equipment hangs on, get drawn for the house and made for it.

The open question is not demand. The houses are being drawn for the room whether or not we build it. It is whether this becomes a contract business or stays a project business, and every decision in the plan is aimed at that.

Working capital comes down to one line in the contract. Equipment on an average project costs us sixty one thousand four hundred dollars, and a fifty percent deposit is five hundred short of the order. So the deposit is fifty five percent, and the equipment is paid for before it is ordered. That holds only if a maker will open an account. Several will not for a dealer with no history, and prepayment plus freight ahead of the second draw is the one real exposure in the model. It is what the sixty thousand reserve is for.

Whether this needs a second round: on these numbers, no. Year 3 pays for itself and Year 4 pays the hole back. A second round would mean the Northeast moving earlier than Year 2 or a third crew sooner than the table carries, and both are decisions we would bring to you rather than discover.

Still ours to settle, and we would rather say so than put a number on a page we have not earned: the structure, the percentage, and what a return looks like. The amount and what it does are above. The terms are a conversation.

The stages

The first job is to make something worth photographing.

  2. 2027

  3. 2028

    Grow the trade program toward twenty five active Texas accounts. Sign audio visual integrators on a referral, which puts us inside houses that are already being wired. Publish everything that went into one finished room, every maker and the reason for each piece.

Risks

What we would ask us about.

Anybody looking at this seriously will find the following, so here it is from us first.

  • Two people and nothing finished. Every claim here is a point of view until the first two rooms exist. That is why year one is built around getting them made and photographed.
  • Those two rooms are sold at cost. Ninety seven thousand dollars of margin given up on purpose, inside the Year 1 line rather than hidden outside the table.
  • The Year 5 numbers assume a third install crew we have not priced. Our own figure for a crew is two hundred and fifty to three hundred and fifty thousand a year, which would take Year 5 EBITDA nearer $1.1M and the valuation band down with it. Treat $1.42M as the top of the range.
  • Nothing is signed with a maker yet. The whole model rests on a 25 to 35 percent dealer margin and we hold none of it today. Makers commonly want a showroom, a territory commitment or a dealer history first, and we have none of the three. If Year 1 lands at fifteen percent instead, this plan does not work at this raise.
  • The multiple is bought by the Northeast and by hiring, not by the care plan. We used to say the care plan was the valuation. It is seven point four percent of Year 5 revenue, so if either of the other two slips there is nothing underneath to catch it.
  • Six projects in Year 1 means every client is seventeen percent of the year. Any one client above ten percent of revenue is on the same list that prices the multiple, and the selling through designers concentrates it further.
  • The growth table assumes the Northeast from Year 2 and our own stage list does not reach it until Year 3. That is a real disagreement inside our plan and we have not resolved it. Until the second market opens this is a bet on North Texas building, and if that slows in 2028 the plan slows with it.
  • The install is the promise. The moment it is handed to whoever is free that week, the thing we are selling stops being true. Crews have to grow before markets do, which is the argument for Texas first.
  • Somebody assembles this before we do. The New York firm already has most of the gym side. If it added wellness and turned to the trade it would be in front of us, and it has four showrooms and we have none.

It is still on at ten at night.
